# Abneos Copy & Conversion Standard

## Purpose
Mandatory standard for material customer-facing copy and conversion work across Abneos projects. Apply to websites, landing pages, funnels, forms, quizzes, email campaigns, adverts, social campaigns, directory conversion journeys and significant copy changes.

## 1. Buyer first
Before writing, establish:
- who the reader is;
- what they want;
- what is frustrating, costly, slow or risky now;
- the outcome they want;
- what prevents action;
- the language they use.

Write to one specific person with one specific problem or desire.

## 2. Outcome before mechanism
Lead with the result the buyer wants. Explain the service, process, platform or feature after the value is clear. Use "you" more than "we" where natural. Convert brand claims into reader benefits.

## 3. Headline and subheading
The headline must earn attention by signalling the desired result, audience, problem/contrast or reason to continue. The subheading must clarify the offer, audience, mechanism and next step without merely repeating the headline.

## 4. Six-lever test
Review major sections against:
1. Attention — does the right reader pause?
2. Me-centric — is it about the reader's needs?
3. Emotion — does it reflect a meaningful desire or pain?
4. Contrast — does it show why the current way is insufficient?
5. Visual language — can the reader picture the situation/result?
6. Tangibility — are there concrete details, proof, examples or specifics?

Rewrite material sections that fail several levers.

## 5. Conversion momentum
For full service/sales pages, normally guide the reader through:
Hero → trust cue → benefits → problem → solution → how it works → proof → comparison where useful → offer details → objections → clear CTA.
Adapt this to the format; do not force long-page structure onto short content.

## 6. Benefits before features
Use: Feature → practical benefit → real-world outcome.
Never leave a material feature unexplained.

## 7. Clarity and friction
A stranger should quickly understand what is offered, who it is for, the problem/result, what happens after clicking and the next step.
Answer relevant friction questions: time, price, commitment, suitability, requirements, human contact and what happens next.
Prefer plain language over insider terminology.

## 8. Proof
Place proof close to the claim it supports. Use only genuine evidence: testimonials, outcomes, screenshots, demonstrations, case studies, certifications, expertise, transparent process/pricing, independent reviews or meaningful first-party data.
Never invent proof, numbers, reviews, outcomes or credentials.

## 9. Calls to action
Prefer action-led CTAs that explain what the user receives or what happens next. Avoid vague labels such as "Submit", "Click here" and generic "Learn more" where a more specific action is available. Add reassurance when it materially reduces uncertainty.

## 10. Design and mobile
Every visual/interactivity choice should help understanding or action. Prioritise rapid comprehension, clear hierarchy, scannability, readable type, strong contrast, tap-friendly controls, minimal form fields and fast mobile performance. Avoid decoration or interaction that obstructs the journey.

## 11. Authenticity / AI commodity test
Ask: "Could essentially the same customer-facing content be generated for a competitor by changing the company name?"
If yes, strengthen it where evidence is genuinely available with:
- first-party data;
- customer questions;
- real screenshots;
- demonstrations;
- specific examples;
- proprietary processes;
- local knowledge;
- founder/team expertise;
- verified customer outcomes;
- original research;
- real experience or observations.
Never manufacture differentiation or evidence to pass this test.

## 12. Format-specific application
- Website/service/sales page: full standard.
- Landing page/quiz/funnel: full standard with extra emphasis on conversion, friction and next-step clarity.
- Email: buyer, outcome, emotion, tangibility, proof where relevant, CTA.
- Social/ad: attention, audience, outcome, contrast, tangibility, CTA.
- Directory/listing conversion copy: clarity, outcome, proof and CTA.

## 13. Definition of Done
For material customer-facing changes, completion requires:
### Technical gate
Lint/typecheck/tests/build as applicable.
### Customer-experience gate
Mobile usability, accessibility, speed, functional forms/CTAs and no internal/admin language exposed to customers.
### Copy/conversion gate
Buyer, outcome, clarity, proof, objections where relevant, CTA, six-lever test and authenticity check.

## 14. Required execution loop
Audit → identify material deficiencies → fix deficiencies that can safely be fixed from available evidence → re-audit.

Do not merely report a fixable deficiency. Escalate only when correction requires missing facts, customer evidence, pricing/offer decisions, legal/compliance judgement or other information that must not be invented.

## 15. Audit output
Use PASS / WARNING / FAIL rather than arbitrary numerical scores.
- FAIL: material conversion, trust, clarity, factual/proof or usability issue that should be corrected before completion.
- WARNING: meaningful improvement opportunity that does not necessarily block completion.
- PASS: standard adequately met.

Minimum audit:
- Target audience
- Desired outcome
- Headline/lead attention
- Offer clarity
- Me-centric language
- Benefits before features
- Emotional relevance where appropriate
- Specific/tangible language
- Proof near material claims
- Objections where relevant
- CTA specificity
- CTA friction
- Mobile comprehension
- Authentic/first-party differentiation
- No invented evidence

## 16. Human validation
For high-value pages/campaigns, seek feedback from real ideal customers, past customers or qualified prospects when practical. Repeated customer language should inform subsequent revisions.

## Core principle
Make the right person feel understood, show a believable better outcome, prove the offer can help, and make the next step simple.
